Field Medical Highlights — XCOPRI (cenobamate)
Date: September 15, 2025   |   From: Rachel Suh, Tom Alvarez

FIELD MEDICAL INSIGHTS

Susan Ratliff, MD, Harborlight Neurology, Portland, ME (AR)
> A generous forty minutes despite a full schedule, and most of it spent on the older half of the panel, which is a large share of this practice.
> The main hesitation with elderly patients is drug-drug interactions on top of already long medication lists.
> Explained that the pharmacy review usually happens after the visit rather than during it, so a decision often waits a week, and asked very politely whether anything shorter than the label section exists that intake staff could work from.
> I committed only to checking what may be shared and to bringing it at the next appt.

Daniel Whitcomb, MD, Northbrook Neurology Institute, Albany, NY (RSM)
> The transition-age population came up first, and without any prompting from me.
> Keeps a running list of 15- and 16-year-olds with drug-resistant focal seizures who simply have to wait until 18 before cenobamate is even an option.
> Was careful to frame this as a limit of what is approved rather than a request to work around it, and asked that the observation be carried back internally; I said I would log it as stated.
> Referrals from two regional pediatric practices have increased this year, which is part of why that list keeps growing.

Mei-Lin Tsang, MD, Bayview Epilepsy Associates, Oakland, CA (RSM)
> A short conversation in the corridor between teaching sessions, and entirely about the academic calendar.
> Two residents rotate onto the epilepsy service in October, and the fellowship journal club is moving to a Thursday evening slot so that the trainees on nights can still attend.
> Asked whether I had recieved the updated rotation schedule from the program office, which I had not; that one is on me to chase.
> No clinical or product discussion took place and nothing was requested.

Grace Lindqvist, MD, Riverstone Medical Group, Hartford, CT (RSM)
> Fifteen minutes between clinics, which the office kindly made room for at short notice.
> Asked directly whether any adolescent dosing or safety data for cenobamate is on the horizon.
> I explained that I am able to share published information within the approved adult indication for XCOPRI only, and that I would not speculate beyond it; the answer was accepted without pushback.
> Open on my side: confirm with Medical Information what may be sent, and reply before the next visit.
